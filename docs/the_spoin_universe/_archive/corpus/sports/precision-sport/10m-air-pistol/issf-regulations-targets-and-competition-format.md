## Target Geometry, Metric Dimensions, and the Decimal Scoring Ring Structure

The International Shooting Sport Federation (ISSF) establishes precise metric specifications for the 10-meter air pistol target under Rule 6.3. The target is positioned at a calibrated distance of exactly 10.00 meters (± 0.05 m) measured horizontally from the firing line to the front face of the target card, with the center of the target mounted at a standard height of 1.40 meters (± 0.05 m) above the level of the firing point.

The target card presents a visible face measuring at least 170 mm x 170 mm. It consists of ten concentric circular scoring zones numbered 1 through 10:
- The Black Aiming Area: Scoring rings 7, 8, 9, and 10 are printed in deep matte black to create a distinct visual aiming bullseye. The diameter of this black circle is exactly 59.5 mm (± 0.5 mm). Rings 1 through 6 are printed on the light cream or white perimeter card.
- Scoring Ring Diameters:
  - 10-ring: 11.5 mm (± 0.1 mm)
  - 9-ring: 27.5 mm (± 0.2 mm)
  - 8-ring: 43.5 mm (± 0.2 mm)
  - 7-ring: 59.5 mm (± 0.5 mm)
  - 6-ring: 75.5 mm (± 0.5 mm)
  - 5-ring: 91.5 mm (± 0.5 mm)
  - 4-ring: 107.5 mm (± 0.5 mm)
  - 3-ring: 123.5 mm (± 0.5 mm)
  - 2-ring: 139.5 mm (± 0.5 mm)
  - 1-ring: 155.5 mm (± 0.5 mm)
- Radial Ring Step: The diameter increases by exactly 16.0 mm per ring, corresponding to a uniform scoring zone radial width of 8.0 mm.
- The Inner Ten (X-Ring): An inner concentric circle measuring 5.0 mm (± 0.1 mm) in diameter. Hits touching or inside this ring are scored as "Inner Tens" (X-counts), used primarily to break ties in integer-scored qualification matches.

In modern competition, electronic scoring subdivides each integer ring into ten concentric decimal rings (Zehntelwertung), from .0 to .9. A shot whose center strikes the target coordinates at dead-center is scored as a 10.9, while a shot that grazes the outer edge of the 11.5 mm 10-ring earns a 10.0.

## Electronic Scoring Target Operating Principles and Calibration

All ISSF World Cups, World Championships, and Olympic Games mandate the use of certified Electronic Scoring Targets (EST), primarily manufactured by SIUS Ascor, Meyton, or Disag. Electronic targets eliminate human scoring discrepancies, deliver instantaneous digital feedback to the athlete's monitor within 100 milliseconds, and measure impact coordinates with a spatial resolution of 0.1 mm.

Two primary technological architectures govern EST systems:
1. Acoustic EST Systems (SIUS Ascor): The target consists of a motorized rubber target band stretched across an acoustically sealed detection chamber. When a 4.5 mm diabolo pellet pierces the rubber band, the micro-shockwave generated by the projectile is detected by four precision piezoelectric transducers mounted at the corners of the sound chamber. A high-speed digital microprocessor calculates the arrival time differences ($\Delta t$) of the acoustic wavefront across the sensor array, triangulating the exact $X$ and $Y$ coordinates of the projectile's passage.
2. Optical EST Systems (Meyton, Disag): The target plane is bounded by dense arrays of infrared light-emitting diodes (LEDs) and opposing phototransistors that project an invisible infrared light grid crossing the aperture in two perpendicular planes. When the lead pellet passes through the measurement plane, it interrupts specific light beams. High-speed optical scanning processors determine the geometric centroid of the pellet's shadow.

Under ISSF rules, electronic targets undergo rigorous pre-competition temperature compensation and calibration routines to ensure that acoustic velocity shifts caused by ambient range temperature variations do not introduce measurement drift.

## Equipment Control Inspections, Dimensional Box, and Dead-Weight Verification

Before an athlete is permitted to step to the firing point in an ISSF-sanctioned match, their firearm and competitive clothing must pass mandatory Equipment Control under ISSF Rule 8.4:
- The Standard Measurement Box: The complete pistol—configured with its sights, cocking levers, balance weights, and anatomical grip in its maximum extended position—must fit completely inside a standardized rectangular inspection box with internal dimensions of:
  - Length: 420 mm
  - Height: 200 mm
  - Depth: 50 mm
  The lid of the box must close flush and latch without requiring physical force or bending any component.
- Maximum Mass Limitation: The total weight of the unloaded pistol, including all attached barrel counterweights and accessories, cannot exceed 1,500 grams (1.5 kg).
- The 500-Gram Trigger Pull Check: An official 500-gram dead-weight equipped with a rubber knife-edge hook is placed on the middle of the trigger blade while the pistol is held vertically pointing upward. The weight must be lifted cleanly off the inspection table without the sear releasing. If the trigger trips and drops the weight, the athlete is denied entry until the trigger spring resistance is adjusted and re-inspected.
- Loading and Propulsion: Only 4.5 mm (.177 caliber) single-shot pre-charged pneumatic or CO2 pistols are permitted. Multi-shot magazines or repeaters are strictly prohibited; each pellet must be manually loaded directly into the breech chamber one at a time. Only open, non-optical metallic sights without magnification or lenses on the firearm are permitted.

## Qualification Match Regulations and Time-Management Protocols

The standard Olympic qualification match for 10-meter air pistol consists of a 60-shot course of fire for both men and women, governed by ISSF Rule 8.7:
- Preparation and Sighting Time: Athletes are granted a 15-minute preparation period prior to the start of competition. During this window, athletes may set up spotting scopes, adjust shooting glasses, and fire an unlimited number of sighting shots (sighters) to confirm electronic target zero and calibrate their holding arc.
- Competition Match Duration: Once the Chief Range Officer commands "MATCH FIRING... START," the 60 competition shots must be completed within a strict time limit of 75 minutes on electronic scoring targets (or 90 minutes if paper targets are utilized).
- Integer Qualification Scoring: Unlike the finals, the 60-shot qualification match is scored in full integer rings (maximum possible qualification score: 600 points). A shot breaking the 11.5 mm ring boundary scores 10, regardless of whether it is a 10.0 or a 10.9. World-class qualification scores typically range from 582 to 592 points.

Effective time management is a critical tactical discipline. Spending 75 minutes to execute 60 shots allows an average of 1 minute and 15 seconds per shot, which accommodates the 25-second physical shot cycle, recovery breaks, and multiple necessary aborts without forcing the athlete into time-pressure rushes during the final ten shots.

## The Olympic Finals Elimination Protocol and High-Pressure Match Dynamics

The top eight athletes from the qualification round advance to the Olympic Final. Under modern ISSF regulations, qualification scores do not carry forward; all eight finalists begin the final from a zero score. The final is conducted with live athlete commentary, music, and audible audience cheering, creating an intense sensory and psychological environment.

The final utilizes decimal scoring (Zehntelwertung) for all shots, where each single shot is scored from 0.0 to 10.9, allowing a theoretical maximum score of 261.6 points over 24 shots:
- Stage 1 (Initial Series): All eight finalists fire two 5-shot series. Each 5-shot series must be completed within a time limit of 250 seconds (10 shots total per athlete).
- Stage 2 (Progressive Elimination): Competition transitions immediately to single elimination shots, with each single shot fired on command within a 50-second window:
  - After shot 12 (two single shots following Stage 1), the 8th-place athlete with the lowest cumulative score is eliminated.
  - Eliminations continue every two shots thereafter:
    - Shot 14: 7th place eliminated
    - Shot 16: 6th place eliminated
    - Shot 18: 5th place eliminated
    - Shot 20: 4th place eliminated
    - Shot 22: 3rd place eliminated (Bronze Medal awarded)
  - Shots 23 and 24: The two remaining athletes fire the final two single shots to decide the Gold and Silver Olympic Medals (24 competition shots total).

In the event of a tied score at any elimination threshold, tied athletes fire sudden-death single shoot-off shots until the tie is broken.
