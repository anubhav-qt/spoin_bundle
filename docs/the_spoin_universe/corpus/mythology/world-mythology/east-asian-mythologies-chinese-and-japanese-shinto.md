# East Asian Mythologies: Chinese and Japanese Shinto

East Asian mythologies articulate cosmic order through harmonious complementary dualities, ancestral veneration, and the sacred sacralization of natural topography. In Chinese tradition, cosmological narratives merge Daoist, Confucian, and Buddhist syntheses within an imperial celestial bureaucracy. In Japanese Shinto tradition, indigenous mytho-histories (*Kojiki* and *Nihon Shoki*) root the sacred lineage of the Imperial Yamato dynasty in nature spirits (*kami*) and cosmic purification rites.

## Chinese Cosmogony: Hundun and the Dismemberment of Pangu

Early Chinese cosmological texts (such as the *Huainanzi* and the *Classic of Mountains and Seas*) describe creation not as a divine command out of nothingness, but as the self-organizing differentiation of primordial chaos (**Hundun**):

In the beginning, the cosmos existed as an undifferentiated, dark, formless cosmic egg. Within this mist rested the giant **Pangu**, who slumbered and grew for eighteen thousand years. Awakening, Pangu seized a great axe and cleaved the cosmic egg open. The light, pure, translucent essence ascended to become **Heaven** (*Tian*, Yang); the heavy, turbid, dark essence sank downward to become **Earth** (*Di*, Yin).

Fearing that heaven and earth might collapse back into chaos, Pangu stood between them, using his head to push heaven upward and his feet to brace earth downward, expanding by ten feet every day for another eighteen thousand years until the two realms were permanently stabilized.

Exhausted, Pangu collapsed and died, transforming his physical anatomy into the geomorphic features of the world:
- His breath became the wind and swirling clouds; his voice the rolling thunder.
- His left eye became the burning Sun; his right eye the pale Moon.
- His blood formed the great rivers and oceans (the Yangtze and Yellow rivers); his flesh the fertile agricultural soil; his hair and beard the stars; his bones the mineral ores and jade.
- His sweat descended as rain and dew, and the tiny parasites and mites dwelling upon his skin transformed into the ancestors of common human beings.

## Nuwa: The Creation of Humanity and the Repair of Heaven

The serpentine mother goddess **Nuwa** (often depicted with a human torso and a sinuous dragon tail intertwined with her consort Fuxi) walked across the newly stabilized earth:

Finding the world desolate, Nuwa knelt beside the Yellow River and began sculpting figures out of yellow river clay in her own image, endowing them with legs to walk upright. As she breathed upon them, the clay figures came to life, laughing and speaking, forming the first humans.

Realizing that sculpting every human by hand was too slow to populate the earth, Nuwa dragged a straw rope through the riverbed mud and swung it through the air. The drops of mud splattering across the earth instantly sprang up as living people. Tradition holds that the hand-sculpted yellow clay humans became nobles, scholars, and rulers, while the mud droplets became the common peasantry.

### Mending the Cracked Sky

Later, the water god **Gonggong** contested the fire god Zhurong for cosmic supremacy. Defeated, Gonggong flew into a violent rage and smashed his head against **Mount Buzhou**, the sacred mountain supporting the northwest pillar of heaven. The pillar snapped, cracking the celestial vault and tearing great rifts in the earth. The sky tilted to the northwest, fire blazed uncontrollably, and subterranean floods threatened to drown mankind.

Nuwa intervened to save her human children. She collected stones of five colors (red, yellow, blue, white, black) from riverbeds, smelted them in a blazing crucible, and used the molten stone paste to patch the fissures in the sky. She then captured a colossal celestial sea turtle (**Ao**), severed its four legs, and erected them at the four cardinal points to serve as eternal pillars holding up heaven. Finally, she burned reeds to ash to block the rising floods, restoring cosmic equilibrium.

## The Celestial Bureaucracy and Syncretic Traditions

Unique to Chinese mythology is the structural organization of heaven as an **Imperial Celestial Bureaucracy**, reflecting the administrative statecraft of dynastic China:

- **The Jade Emperor (Yu Huang):** The supreme monarch of heaven, presiding over celestial ministries (the Ministry of Thunder, the Ministry of Fire, the Ministry of Wealth, and the Ministry of Epidemics). Divine promotions, demotions, and assignments are recorded in celestial registers based on moral merit.
- **The Kitchen God (Zao Jun):** A localized domestic deity installed above the cooking hearth of every household. He records the family's daily conduct, arguments, and virtues, ascending to heaven once a year prior to the Lunar New Year to present a dossier to the Jade Emperor. Families offer him sticky sweet rice cakes to sweeten his words or seal his mouth shut.
- **Yanluo Wang and the Ten Courts of Hell (*Diyu*):** The sovereign of the underworld who judges deceased souls according to their earthly deeds, subjecting them to specific retributive punishments in eighteen levels of hell before they drink the tea of forgetfulness brewed by Lady Meng and enter the wheel of reincarnation.
- **Daoist-Buddhist Syncretism (The Monkey King):** The cultural epic *Journey to the West* demonstrates the late imperial integration of Daoist magic, Confucian duty, and Buddhist salvation. The irreverent monkey immortal **Sun Wukong**, born from a mystical stone, masters 72 transformations and somersaults across the clouds, defying the entire Jade Emperor's army before the Buddha pins him beneath the Mountain of Five Elements, eventually redeeming himself by escorting the monk Xuanzang to India.

## Japanese Shinto Myth: The Kojiki and the Sacred Islands

Japanese mythology, codified in the **Kojiki** (*Records of Ancient Matters*, 712 CE) and **Nihon Shoki** (*Chronicles of Japan*, 720 CE), anchors the indigenous animistic tradition of **Shinto** (the Way of the Gods):

The celestial deities dispatched the primeval divine couple, **Izanagi** (The Male Who Invites) and **Izanami** (The Female Who Invites), to solidify the drifting, chaotic earth. Standing upon the Floating Bridge of Heaven (*Ame-no-Ukihashi*), they thrust the Jeweled Spear of Heaven (*Ame-no-Nuboko*) into the brine below. When they withdrew the spear, the brine dripping from its tip coagulated to form the sacred island of **Onogoro**.

Descending to Onogoro, they erected the Heavenly Pillar and instituted the marriage ritual by walking in opposite directions around the pillar. When they met, Izanami spoke first: *"Ah, what a fair youth!"* Because the female spoke first, the cosmic order was violated, and their firstborn was born deformed—the Leech Child (**Hiruko**)—who was placed in a reed boat and cast adrift. Correcting the ritual with Izanagi speaking first, Izanami gave birth to the eight major islands of Japan (**Oyashima**) and the sacred nature spirits (**kami**) animating mountains, waterfalls, trees, and wind.

## Izanami's Descent to Yomi and the Rites of Misogi Purification

When giving birth to the fire god **Kagutsuchi**, Izanami was fatally burned and descended into **Yomi**, the shadowy subterranean realm of the dead.

Grief-stricken, Izanagi traveled to Yomi to retrieve his wife. Meeting in the gloom, Izanami agreed to appeal to the underworld lords, warning Izanagi never to look upon her. Growing impatient in the darkness, Izanagi snapped a tooth from his comb and lit it as a torch. He recoiled in horror: Izanami's body was a decomposing corpse, crawling with maggots and eight monstrous thunder gods.

Humiliated and enraged, Izanami dispatched the hags of Yomi and a horde of warriors to pursue him. Izanagi fled to the pass of Yomotsu Hirasaka, blocking the entrance with a massive boulder. From across the stone, Izanami cursed him, declaring she would strangle one thousand of his people every day; Izanagi retorted that he would cause fifteen hundred children to be born each day, ensuring the triumph of life over death.

Tainted by the deathly defilement (*kegare*) of Yomi, Izanagi went to a river in Kyushu to perform **Misogi** (ritual ablution):
- Washing his **left eye** gave birth to **Amaterasu Omikami**, the glorious Sun Goddess.
- Washing his **right eye** gave birth to **Tsukuyomi no Mikoto**, the Moon God.
- Washing his **nose** gave birth to **Susanoo no Mikoto**, the tempestuous God of Storms and the Sea.

## Amaterasu, the Heavenly Rock Cave, and the Imperial Regalia

Susanoo behaved with destructive arrogance, rampaging through heaven, destroying Amaterasu's irrigated rice paddies, and hurling a flayed celestial horse through the roof of her sacred weaving hall. Terrified and grieved, Amaterasu retreated into the **Heavenly Rock Cave** (*Ame-no-Iwato*), rolling a great boulder to seal the entrance.

Instantly, the High Plain of Heaven (*Takamagahara*) and the earthly realms were plunged into pitch darkness; evil spirits ran rampant, and agricultural life withered.

The eight hundred myriad *kami* assembled in the dry riverbed outside the cave. To coax her out, they hung the **Yata no Kagami** (the Sacred Bronze Mirror) and curved jewels on a sacred sakaki tree. The comedic fertility goddess **Ame-no-Uzume** overturned a wooden tub, stepped upon it, and danced an ecstatic, ribald dance, baring her breasts and thighs. The divine assembly burst into roaring laughter.

Intrigued that the gods were laughing in a universe enveloped in darkness, Amaterasu opened the cave door a crack. Uzume called out that a goddess more radiant than Amaterasu had arrived, directing her gaze toward the bronze mirror. Mesmerized by her own luminous reflection, Amaterasu stepped forward. The strong-armed god Tajikarao seized her hand, pulling her out, while a sacred straw rope (**shimenawa**) was stretched across the cave mouth, permanently restoring light to the universe.

### The Slaying of Yamata no Orochi and the Imperial Regalia

Banished from heaven to the earthly province of Izumo, Susanoo redeemed himself by rescuing a maiden from the terrifying **Yamata no Orochi**, an eight-headed, eight-tailed dragon that drank eight vats of refined sake. After the serpent fell into a drunken stupor, Susanoo hacked it to pieces. Inside the beast's tail, his sword struck a hard object: the sacred blade **Kusanagi no Tsurugi** (the Grass-Cutting Sword), which he presented to Amaterasu.

Amaterasu later dispatched her grandson, **Ninigi no Mikoto**, to rule the earthly realm of Japan, bestowing the **Three Sacred Imperial Regalia**:
1. The **Mirror** (*Yata no Kagami*), embodying truth and divine wisdom.
2. The **Jewel** (*Yasakani no Magatama*), embodying benevolence.
3. The **Sword** (*Kusanagi no Tsurugi*), embodying martial valor.

Ninigi's great-grandson, **Emperor Jimmu** (traditional accession 660 BCE), became the first Emperor of Japan, establishing the sacred mythological foundation of the Imperial House of Japan.
