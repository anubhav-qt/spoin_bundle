## Foundations of Diffusion: DDPM, Markov Chains, and Denoising
Generative visual synthesis underwent a foundational revolution through Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM), established by Jonathan Ho, Ajay Jain, and Pieter Abbeel in 2020, drawing on non-equilibrium thermodynamics. The diffusion framework conceptualizes generation as the reversal of a forward noise-degradation process. In the **Forward Process**, a clean training image $\mathbf{x}_0$ is systematically corrupted across $T$ discrete timesteps by injecting Gaussian noise through a predefined Markov chain governed by variance schedule $\beta_1, \dots, \beta_T$: $q(\mathbf{x}_t \mid \mathbf{x}_{t-1}) = \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}_t; \sqrt{1 - \beta_t}\mathbf{x}_{t-1}, \beta_t \mathbf{I})$. A key mathematical property of Gaussian distributions permits analytical sampling of any arbitrary timestep $t$ in a single closed-form step: $q(\mathbf{x}_t \mid \mathbf{x}_0) = \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}_t; \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}\mathbf{x}_0, (1 - \bar{\alpha}_t)\mathbf{I})$, where $\alpha_t = 1 - \beta_t$ and $\bar{\alpha}_t = \prod_{s=1}^t \alpha_s$. In the **Reverse Process**, a neural network parameterized by weights $\theta$ learns to invert this entropy degradation. The network is trained to predict the exact noise vector $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}$ injected into corrupted image $\mathbf{x}_t$ by minimizing the simplified mean squared error loss: $L(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t, \mathbf{x}_0, \boldsymbol{\epsilon}} \left[ \|\boldsymbol{\epsilon} - \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t)\|^2 \right]$. During inference, generation begins from pure Gaussian noise $\mathbf{x}_T \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})$, iteratively subtracting predicted noise increments over hundreds of timesteps to reveal a high-fidelity synthetic image.

## Deterministic Sampling and Latent Diffusion Models (LDM)
While foundational DDPM achieved unmatched visual fidelity and diversity, its reliance on hundreds of stochastic Markovian iterations made inference prohibitively slow, requiring up to 1,000 forward passes per generated image. The first major breakthrough arrived with Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIM), formulated by Jiaming Song and colleagues. DDIM generalizes diffusion to a family of non-Markovian forward processes that share the exact same marginal distributions as DDPM, enabling deterministic reverse sampling trajectories. By treating the reverse diffusion process as an ordinary differential equation (ODE), DDIM allows numerical solvers to skip intermediate timesteps, slashing generation time from 1,000 steps down to 20 to 50 steps without retrained weights. The second major architectural breakthrough was the Latent Diffusion Model (LDM), established by Robin Rombach and Patrick Esser (which became the foundation for Stable Diffusion). Performing diffusion directly in high-dimensional pixel space ($H \times W \times 3$) is computationally ruinous because millions of floating-point operations are wasted modeling imperceptible high-frequency pixel noise. LDM decouples perceptual compression from semantic generation using a pre-trained Variational Autoencoder (VAE). The VAE encoder compresses high-resolution images by an $8\times$ downsampling factor into a compact, continuous latent space ($\mathbf{z} = \mathcal{E}(\mathbf{x})$); the diffusion network is trained entirely within this low-dimensional latent manifold, and the final latent is decoded back to RGB pixels ($\mathbf{x} = \mathcal{D}(\mathbf{z})$), cutting computational requirements by orders of magnitude.

## The Continuous Dynamics Paradigm: Flow Matching and Rectified Flow
Despite the efficiency of latent diffusion, classical diffusion models suffer from a fundamental mathematical inefficiency: they map noise to data along highly curved probability trajectories, which induces numerical truncation drift and necessitates multi-step ODE solvers. In modern generative architectures (such as FLUX.1 and Stable Diffusion 3), classical diffusion has been superseded by **Flow Matching** and **Rectified Flow**, formulated by Yaron Lipman and Xingchao Liu. Flow Matching frames generative modeling as learning Continuous Normalizing Flows (CNFs) governed by time-dependent vector fields: $\frac{d \mathbf{x}_t}{d t} = \mathbf{v}_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t)$. Instead of following stochastic curved noise schedules, Flow Matching defines deterministic, straight-line probability paths connecting the source Gaussian noise distribution $\mathbf{x}_0$ directly to the target data distribution $\mathbf{x}_1$: $\mathbf{x}_t = (1 - t)\mathbf{x}_0 + t \mathbf{x}_1$ for $t \in [0, 1]$. The neural network is trained with a simple, robust regression objective, learning to predict the constant straight velocity vector: $\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{x}_1 - \mathbf{x}_0$. Rectified Flow further refines this through a "reflow" procedure, iteratively straightening trajectories across the data manifold. Because paths are linear, numerical integration solvers require minimal sub-steps, enabling high-fidelity image generation in as few as 1 to 4 steps with unmatched prompt adherence.

## Diffusion Transformers (DiT): Replacing U-Nets with Attention
For nearly a decade, visual generative modeling relied almost exclusively on convolutional U-Net backbones equipped with downsampling blocks, residual convolutions, and cross-attention bridges. In 2023, William Peebles and Saining Xie introduced Diffusion Transformers (DiT), demonstrating that convolutional U-Nets were not an inherent architectural necessity for diffusion and that Transformers represent a superior, more scalable backbone. DiT eliminates convolutions entirely, operating on spatial latents using standard Vision Transformer (ViT) mechanics. The input latent tensor is "patchified"—sliced into non-overlapping spatial patches of size $p \times p$ (typically $2 \times 2$), flattened, and linearly projected into a continuous sequence of tokens embedded with spatial positional vectors. Conditioning information—including diffusion timestep $t$, class labels, and text prompt embeddings—is injected via **Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN-Zero)**. Instead of appending tokens, an auxiliary multi-layer perceptron regresses dimension-wise scale ($\gamma$), shift ($\beta$), and residual gate ($\alpha$) parameters directly from conditioning embeddings: $\text{AdaLN}(\mathbf{h}) = \gamma(t, c) \odot \text{LayerNorm}(\mathbf{h}) + \beta(t, c)$. Crucially, AdaLN-Zero initializes these gating parameters to zero, causing every residual block to function as an identity mapping at initialization, which dramatically stabilizes deep model scaling.

## State-of-the-Art Visual Synthesis: FLUX.1, SD3, and Video Generation
By marrying Diffusion Transformers (DiT) with Rectified Flow matching, modern frontier visual models have achieved unprecedented levels of anatomical precision, photorealistic texture fidelity, and typographic legibility. Models like Black Forest Labs' **FLUX.1** (a 12-billion-parameter hybrid model) utilize dual-stream and single-stream DiT blocks with Multimodal Rotary Position Embeddings (MM-RoPE), allowing visual latent tokens and text tokens from T5-XXL and CLIP encoders to attend to one another in a unified representational space. This architecture enables flawless text rendering within generated images, complex multi-subject spatial compositions, and photographic lighting that reacts naturally to material roughness. Furthermore, this exact architectural paradigm scales directly to temporal video generation. State-of-the-art video models—such as OpenAI's Sora, Runway's Gen-3, Meta's Movie Gen, and Kling—extend DiT to three-dimensional Spatio-Temporal Patches. By compressing raw video frames across both space ($8\times$) and time ($4\times$) using 3D Video VAEs, video DiT models process spacetime cuboids as token sequences, generating minutes of temporally coherent, high-definition video with persistent physical object permanence, fluid dynamics, and realistic dynamic camera movements.
