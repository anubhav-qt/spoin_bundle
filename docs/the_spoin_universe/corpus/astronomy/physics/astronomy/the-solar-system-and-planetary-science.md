# The Solar System and Planetary Science

## Solar Nebula Collapse and Protoplanetary Disk Evolution

The Solar System formed approximately $4.567\text{ billion years ago}$ from the gravitational collapse of a dense, rotating core within a Giant Molecular Cloud:

- **The Solar Nebula Hypothesis:** As the cloud core collapsed under self-gravity, conservation of angular momentum ($\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{r} \times m\mathbf{v}$) forced the infalling gas and dust to flatten into a circumstellar **protoplanetary disk** rotating around the infant proto-Sun.
- **The Minimum Mass Solar Nebula (MMSN):** A theoretical benchmark constructed by augmenting the present planetary masses with solar-abundance hydrogen and helium to match the Sun's composition. The MMSN estimates a total disk mass of $M_{\text{disk}} \sim 0.01 - 0.05 M_\odot$, with a radial surface density profile scaling as:
$$\Sigma(r) \approx \Sigma_0 \left(\frac{r}{1\text{ AU}}\right)^{-3/2} \quad \text{where} \quad \Sigma_0 \approx 1700\text{ g cm}^{-2}$$
- **Disk Thermal Structure:** Viscous dissipation of accretion energy combined with irradiation from the central proto-Sun created a steep radial temperature gradient:
$$T(r) \propto r^{-1/2} \text{ to } r^{-3/4}$$
Temperatures in the inner disk exceeded $\sim 1500-2000\text{ K}$, vaporizing all pre-existing interstellar dust, while the outer disk remained cold ($T < 50\text{ K}$).

## The Condensation Sequence and Snow Lines

As the protoplanetary disk cooled, chemical species condensed out of the gaseous phase into solid grains at temperatures dictated by their local thermodynamic equilibrium condensation temperatures:

- **The Equilibrium Condensation Sequence:**
  1. *Refractory Oxides and Silicates ($T_{\text{cond}} \sim 1400 - 1800\text{ K}$):* Corundum ($\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$), spinel ($\text{MgAl}_2\text{O}_4$), and perovskite ($\text{CaTiO}_3$), preserved today in Calcium-Aluminum-rich Inclusions (CAIs) found in primitive chondritic meteorites.
  2. *Metallic Iron-Nickel and Silicates ($T_{\text{cond}} \sim 1000 - 1400\text{ K}$):* Iron-nickel metal alloy ($\text{Fe-Ni}$) and magnesium-rich silicates: forsterite ($\text{Mg}_2\text{SiO}_4$) and enstatite ($\text{MgSiO}_3$).
  3. *Moderately Volatiles ($T_{\text{cond}} \sim 400 - 700\text{ K}$):* Troilite ($\text{FeS}$), alkali feldspars, and hydrous clays.
  4. *Volatile Ices ($T_{\text{cond}} < 200\text{ K}$):* Water ice ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$ at $\sim 160-180\text{ K}$), carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$ at $\sim 70\text{ K}$), ammonia ($\text{NH}_3$), methane ($\text{CH}_4$), and carbon monoxide ($\text{CO}$ at $\sim 20-30\text{ K}$).
- **The Water Snow Line:** The radial boundary ($r_{\text{snow}} \sim 2.7\text{ AU}$ in the early solar nebula) where temperatures dropped below $T \approx 160-180\text{ K}$, allowing water vapor to condense into solid water ice.
  - *The Compositional Dichotomy:* Inside the snow line, solid grains consisted solely of rocky silicates and metals, representing only $\sim 0.5\%$ of the local disk mass; planets that formed here (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars) became small, dense **terrestrial worlds**. Beyond the snow line, abundant water ice boosted the solid surface density by a factor of 3–4, allowing massive solid cores ($\sim 10-20 M_\oplus$) to assemble rapidly before disk gas dissipated, capturing massive hydrogen-helium envelopes to become **gas giants** (Jupiter, Saturn) and **ice giants** (Uranus, Neptune).

## From Dust Grains to Planets: Pebble Accretion and Planetesimals

Transforming sub-micron dust grains into $1,000\text{-km}$ planetary embryos requires crossing multiple aerodynamic and gravitational barriers:

- **Dust Coagulation and the Bouncing Barrier:** Microscopic dust grains collide via Brownian motion and van der Waals forces, sticking together into porous, fractal aggregates. However, when particles reach millimeter to centimeter sizes, high collision velocities cause bouncing or catastrophic fragmentation rather than adhesion (**the fragmentation barrier**).
- **Radial Drift and the Meter-Size Barrier:** Gas in the disk is partially supported by outward radial pressure gradients ($dP/dr < 0$), causing gas to orbit at slightly sub-Keplerian velocity ($v_{\text{gas}} < v_K$). Solid pebbles orbit at Keplerian speed; they experience a continuous headwind, losing angular momentum and rapidly spiraling inward into the Sun on timescales of just $\sim 100\text{ years}$ for meter-sized boulders.
- **The Streaming Instability:** Gas drag forces pebbles to concentrate into dense filaments. When local pebble density exceeds gas density, the collective drag of the pebbles accelerates the gas, reducing headwind and causing clumps of pebbles to gravitationally collapse directly into $10-100\text{ km}$ **planetesimals**, bypassing the meter-size barrier in a single step.
- **Pebble Accretion and Embryo Growth:** Once a planetesimal exceeds a critical mass ($M \gtrsim 10^{-4} M_\oplus$), gas drag efficiently captures drifting millimeter-to-centimeter pebbles into its gravitational potential. This **pebble accretion** allows planetary embryos to grow at rates orders of magnitude faster than classical planetesimal-planetesimal collisions, building giant planet cores within the disk's $\sim 3-5\text{ Myr}$ lifetime.
- **Giant Impacts:** In the inner Solar System, oligarchic growth produced $\sim 20-50$ Mars-sized planetary embryos that perturbed each other into crossing orbits, culminating in chaotic, violent **giant impacts** (including the Moon-forming impact on Earth) that cleared remaining debris and merged embryos into the four terrestrial planets.

## Planetary Migration and Dynamical Instabilities

Planets do not necessarily remain at the orbital radii where they accreted; gravitational interactions with the protoplanetary gas disk and remnant planetesimals drive orbital migration:

- **Disk-Driven Migration:**
  - *Type I Migration:* Low-mass planets ($M \lesssim 10 M_\oplus$) launch spiral density waves in the gas disk. Differential Lindblad torques between the inner and outer disk exert a net negative torque, driving rapid inward migration toward the central star on timescales of $\sim 10^5\text{ years}$.
  - *Type II Migration:* Massive gas giants ($M \gtrsim 1 M_{\text{Jupiter}}$) clear an annular gap in the disk. The planet becomes locked within the gap, migrating inward along with the viscous accretion flow of the disk.
- **The Grand Tack Model:** Hypothesizes that Jupiter migrated inward to $\sim 1.5\text{ AU}$ via Type I/II migration, truncating the inner planetesimal disk (explaining the anomalously low mass of Mars). When Saturn formed and entered a 3:2 mean-motion resonance with Jupiter, overlapping gaps reversed the net torque, causing both gas giants to "tack" and migrate outward to their current orbits.
- **The Nice Model of Giant Planet Instability:** Long after the gas disk dissipated ($t \sim 100-500\text{ Myr}$), Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune orbited in a compact resonant configuration surrounded by an outer icy planetesimal disk. Slow planetesimal scattering eventually drove Jupiter and Saturn across their mutual 2:1 mean-motion resonance.
  - *Catastrophic Reconfiguration:* Crossing the 2:1 resonance pumped orbital eccentricities, triggering a chaotic global instability. Uranus and Neptune were scattered outward into the icy disk, scattering Kuiper Belt objects into the inner Solar System (producing the **Late Heavy Bombardment**, LHB, at $\sim 3.9\text{ Ga}$) and carving the modern Kuiper Belt architecture.

## Comparative Planetology: The Global Planetary Engine

Comparative planetology treats planetary bodies as physical heat engines governed by a universal causal cascade:
$$\text{Mass} \longrightarrow \text{Gravity} \longrightarrow \text{Internal Heat Budget} \longrightarrow \text{Differentiation} \longrightarrow \text{Dynamo} \longrightarrow \text{Atmosphere} \longrightarrow \text{Geology} \longrightarrow \text{Climate}$$

- **Mercury ($M = 0.055 M_\oplus$):** Low mass permitted rapid interior cooling. An enormous metallic iron-nickel core ($\sim 85\%$ of planetary radius) generated global contraction scarps (lobate scarps) as it cooled. A weak, active global dipole magnetic field ($\sim 1\%$ of Earth's) is sustained by a partially molten core dynamo. Virtually no atmosphere exists; the surface is an ancient, heavily cratered regolith.
- **Venus ($M = 0.815 M_\oplus$):** Near-Earth twin in mass and radius, but lacking an intrinsic magnetic dynamo (likely due to slow retrograde rotation or stagnant core convection). Suffered a runaway greenhouse effect; high surface temperatures ($T \approx 735\text{ K}$) drove volatile loss and prevented water-assisted plate tectonics, resulting in a **stagnant-lid convection** regime punctuated by periodic global volcanic resurfacing events.
- **Earth ($M = 1.0 M_\oplus$):** Possesses active mobile plate tectonics, a liquid water ocean, and a robust geodynamo. Long-lived radiogenic heating and mantle convection sustain the **carbonate-silicate weathering cycle**, which acts as a planetary thermostat stabilizing surface temperatures over geologic time.
- **Mars ($M = 0.107 M_\oplus$):** Small mass drove rapid thermal cooling. Its core dynamo shut down $\sim 4.0\text{ Ga}$, terminating its protective global magnetosphere. Without magnetic shielding, solar wind stripping thinned its once-dense atmosphere, transforming Mars from a warmer, wetter early environment with flowing surface water into a cold, hyper-arid, oxidized desert.

## The Earth-Moon System: Formation, Tides, and Dynamics

The Earth-Moon system represents an exceptional planetary architecture among the terrestrial worlds:

- **The Giant Impact Hypothesis (Theia):** Approximately $4.51\text{ Ga}$, a differentiated Mars-sized protoplanet (dubbed "Theia") struck the proto-Earth in a grazing, oblique impact:
  - *Impact Physics:* The metallic core of Theia merged into Earth's core, while the vaporized silicate mantles of both bodies were ejected into a circumterrestrial debris disk.
  - *Accretion:* The silicate disk rapidly condensed and accreted into the Moon within months to years, explaining why the Moon is severely depleted in iron, possesses an anomalously low bulk density ($\rho \approx 3.34\text{ g cm}^{-3}$), and shares near-identical oxygen isotope ratios with Earth's mantle ($\Delta^{17}\text{O} \approx 0$).
- **Tidal Recoil and Lunar Recession:** The Moon formed close to Earth ($a \sim 3-5 R_\oplus$), where tidal forces raised enormous bulges on Earth's molten surface. Because Earth rotates faster than the Moon orbits ($\Omega_\oplus > n_{\text{Moon}}$), friction carries the tidal bulge ahead of the Earth-Moon axis. The gravitational pull of this forward-leading bulge exerts a prograde torque on the Moon, transferring angular momentum from Earth's spin to the lunar orbit:
  - *Current Recession Rate:* Lunar Laser Ranging retroreflectors measure the Moon's ongoing outward recession at $3.82 \pm 0.07\text{ cm/year}$.
  - *Day Lengthening:* Earth's rotation period is decelerating at a rate of $\sim 2.3\text{ milliseconds per century}$.
- **Synchronous Rotation and Libration:** Tidal torques rapidly locked the Moon's rotation into a 1:1 spin-orbit resonance, causing it to present the same hemisphere toward Earth. Because the Moon's orbit is elliptical ($e = 0.0549$), its uniform rotational velocity alternately leads and lags its non-uniform orbital speed, producing **optical libration in longitude** that allows Earth-based observers to view $\sim 59\%$ of the lunar surface over an orbital cycle.

## Planetary Interiors: Differentiation, Heat Budgets, and Oceans

Planetary internal evolution is driven by density-driven structural sorting and thermodynamic cooling:

- **Planetary Differentiation:** Early in a terrestrial body's history, gravitational potential energy of accretion combined with decay heat from short-lived radioisotopes (primarily $^{26}\text{Al}$, half-life $0.72\text{ Myr}$) melted the interior. Dense molten metallic iron and nickel sank to the center under gravity to form a metallic core, while lighter silicate minerals floated upward to form a mantle and crust.
- **Heat Sources in Planetary Interiors:**
  1. *Primordial Heat:* Retained gravitational energy of accretion and core separation.
  2. *Radiogenic Decay:* Long-lived radioisotopes distributed in the silicate mantle and crust:
     - Potassium-40 ($^{40}\text{K}$, $\tau_{1/2} = 1.25\text{ Gyr}$)
     - Thorium-232 ($^{232}\text{Th}$, $\tau_{1/2} = 14.0\text{ Gyr}$)
     - Uranium-235 ($^{235}\text{U}$, $\tau_{1/2} = 0.70\text{ Gyr}$)
     - Uranium-238 ($^{238}\text{U}$, $\tau_{1/2} = 4.47\text{ Gyr}$)
  3. *Tidal Dissipation:* Cyclic gravitational flexing in eccentric resonant orbits (e.g., Io, Europa, Enceladus).
- **Subsurface Global Liquid Oceans in Outer Moons:** In the outer Solar System, tidal dissipation and radiogenic heating sustain deep liquid water oceans beneath outer icy shells:
  - *Europa:* A global subsurface liquid water ocean ($100-150\text{ km}$ deep) containing more water than all of Earth's oceans combined, situated directly over a silicate seafloor with potential hydrothermal venting.
  - *Enceladus:* South polar plumes erupt water vapor, ice grains, silica nanoparticles, and simple organic molecules directly into space from a global ocean heated by core hydrothermal activity.
  - *Titan:* Deep subsurface water-ammonia ocean underlying a dense organic crust with methane lakes.

## Planetary Atmospheres: Origins, Circulation, and Greenhouse Thermodynamics

A planet's atmospheric composition and climate reflect competitive generation and loss mechanisms:

- **Primary vs. Secondary Atmospheres:**
  - *Primary Atmospheres:* Primordial hydrogen and helium captured directly from the solar nebula. Only massive gas giants possessed gravity sufficient to retain primary envelopes against thermal escape.
  - *Secondary Atmospheres:* Generated on terrestrial worlds through interior volcanic outgassing ($\text{H}_2\text{O}, \text{CO}_2, \text{N}_2, \text{SO}_2$) and delivered via late cometary and asteroidal bombardments.
- **Greenhouse Thermodynamics:** Solar shortwave radiation passes through transparent atmospheric gases and is absorbed by the planetary surface. The warmed surface re-radiates longwave thermal infrared radiation. Greenhouse gases ($\text{H}_2\text{O}, \text{CO}_2, \text{CH}_4$) absorb infrared photons, re-emitting a fraction back downward and raising surface temperatures:
  - *Earth:* A modest greenhouse warming of $\sim 33\text{ K}$ raises mean surface temperature from $255\text{ K}$ (effective blackbody equilibrium) to $288\text{ K}$ ($+15^\circ\text{C}$), maintaining liquid surface water.
  - *Venus:* An extreme **runaway greenhouse effect**. Venus received solar insolation exceeding the moist greenhouse threshold, vaporizing initial oceans. Water vapor amplified greenhouse heating until oceans fully evaporated; UV photolysis stripped hydrogen into space, leaving a dry, crushing $92\text{ bar}$ $\text{CO}_2$ atmosphere heating the surface to $735\text{ K}$ ($462^\circ\text{C}$), hotter than Mercury's sunlit side.
- **Atmospheric Circulation Regimes:**
  - *Slow Rotators (Venus, Titan):* Low Coriolis parameter ($f = 2\Omega \sin\phi$). Hadley circulation cells extend from the equator all the way to the poles, driving **super-rotation** where upper winds circle the planet in days.
  - *Rapid Rotators (Earth, Jupiter, Saturn):* High Coriolis forces break circulation into multiple latitudinal cells (Hadley, Ferrel, Polar cells on Earth) and alternating eastward/westward zonal jet streams, producing the distinct banded cloud belts and Great Red Spot storm systems on Jupiter.

## Atmospheric Escape Physics: Thermal and Non-Thermal Regimes

A planet's ability to retain an atmosphere against space vacuum depends on the competition between surface gravity and escape processes:

- **Thermal Escape (Jeans Escape):** In an exosphere, gas particles follow a Maxwell-Boltzmann velocity distribution:
$$f(v) dv = 4\pi \left(\frac{m}{2\pi k_B T}\right)^{3/2} v^2 \exp\left(-\frac{m v^2}{2 k_B T}\right) dv$$
Particles in the high-velocity tail exceeding the local escape velocity ($v > v_{\text{esc}} = \sqrt{2GM/R}$) escape into space if they avoid further collisions. The **Jeans escape parameter** is:
$$\lambda_{\text{esc}} = \frac{v_{\text{esc}}^2}{v_{\text{th}}^2} = \frac{G M m}{k_B T R_{\text{exo}}}$$
where $v_{\text{th}} = \sqrt{2k_B T / m}$ is the most probable thermal speed. If $v_{\text{esc}} \lesssim 6 v_{\text{th}}$ ($\lambda_{\text{esc}} \lesssim 15-20$), Jeans escape depletes that gas species over geologic timescales. Because $v_{\text{th}} \propto m^{-1/2}$, light elements (H, He) escape rapidly from terrestrial planets, while heavier molecules ($\text{N}_2, \text{O}_2, \text{CO}_2$) remain gravitationally bound.
- **Hydrodynamic Escape ("Blow-Off"):** Intense extreme-ultraviolet (EUV) solar flux heats the upper atmosphere, driving supersonic bulk fluid expansion that sweeps heavy species away alongside escaping hydrogen.
- **Non-Thermal Escape Regimes (Solar Wind Stripping):**
  - *Charge Exchange:* Solar wind protons capture an electron from an exospheric neutral atom ($p + \text{H} \to \text{H}_{\text{fast}} + p$), creating an energetic neutral atom that escapes magnetic trapping.
  - *Ion Sputtering:* Solar wind ions accelerated by the interplanetary electric field slam into the upper atmosphere, physically knocking atmospheric neutrals into space.
  - *Photoionization and Sweeping:* Solar photons ionize upper atmospheric atoms; the unshielded solar wind magnetic field picks up these ions and sweeps them into the interplanetary medium, a process that stripped Mars of several bars of atmosphere over cosmic time.

## Planetary Magnetospheres and Solar Wind Interactions

Planetary magnetic fields act as primary shields deflect charged space radiation:

- **Planetary Dynamo Generation:** Sustaining an intrinsic global dipole magnetic field requires three conditions within a planetary interior:
  1. A large volume of electrically conducting fluid (liquid iron-nickel in Earth; liquid metallic hydrogen in Jupiter and Saturn; water-ammonia-ion ionic oceans in Uranus and Neptune).
  2. Thermal or compositional buoyancy driving fluid convection.
  3. Planetary rotation providing Coriolis forces to organize convective motions into helical columnar rolls, amplifying and regenerating magnetic fields via the $\alpha\Omega$ dynamo.
- **Magnetosphere Architecture:**
  - *Bow Shock:* The supersonic, super-Alfvénic solar wind ($v \sim 400-800\text{ km s}^{-1}$) encounters the planetary magnetic barrier, decelerating to subsonic speeds at a detached bow shock.
  - *Magnetopause:* The boundary where dynamic solar wind ram pressure balances the internal magnetic pressure of the planet's dipole:
  $$\frac{B^2}{2\mu_0} \approx \rho_{\text{sw}} v_{\text{sw}}^2 \implies R_{\text{mp}} \approx R_p \left(\frac{B_0^2}{2\mu_0 \rho_{\text{sw}} v_{\text{sw}}^2}\right)^{1/6}$$
  - *Radiation Belts (Van Allen Belts):* Toroidal regions where charged solar wind and cosmic ray ions are trapped by magnetic Lorentz forces ($\mathbf{F} = q(\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B})$), executing bounce and drift orbits.
  - *Auroral Acceleration:* Magnetic reconnection events in the extended magnetotail accelerate electrons along magnetic field lines into the polar upper atmosphere. Collisions with atomic oxygen and molecular nitrogen excite electronic states, emitting green ($557.7\text{ nm}$) and red ($630.0\text{ nm}$) auroral light.

## Moons and Satellite Systems of the Outer Planets

The giant planets host diverse satellite systems categorized into regular and irregular populations:

- **Regular vs. Irregular Satellites:**
  - *Regular Satellites:* Large, coplanar, prograde moons on low-eccentricity orbits formed within circumplanetary accretion disks (miniature solar nebulae), exhibiting synchronous rotation (e.g., the Galilean moons, Titan).
  - *Irregular Satellites:* Small, distant moons on highly inclined, eccentric, and often retrograde orbits captured from the Kuiper Belt or asteroid belt during giant-planet migration (e.g., Phoebe, Himalia).
- **The Galilean Satellites of Jupiter:** Exhibit a monotonic density and volatile gradient reflecting Jupiter's circumplanetary thermal gradient:
  - *Io ($\rho \approx 3.53\text{ g cm}^{-3}$):* Anhydrous, silicate-iron moon; tidal heating drives continuous sulfur volcanism.
  - *Europa ($\rho \approx 3.01\text{ g cm}^{-3}$):* Thin ice crust covering a global liquid ocean over a rocky mantle.
  - *Ganymede ($\rho \approx 1.94\text{ g cm}^{-3}$):* The largest moon in the Solar System; fully differentiated with a liquid iron core generating the only known intrinsic lunar magnetic dynamo.
  - *Callisto ($\rho \approx 1.83\text{ g cm}^{-3}$):* Heavily cratered, ancient, undifferentiated mixture of ice and rock.
- **Titan (Saturn):** The only moon possessing a dense atmosphere ($1.5\text{ bar}$, $95\% \text{N}_2, 5\% \text{CH}_4$). Methane acts as the thermodynamic analog of water, undergoing an active hydrologic cycle of methane clouds, rainfall, rivers, and liquid methane-ethane hydrocarbon lakes (Kraken Mare).
- **Triton (Neptune):** A captured Kuiper Belt Object orbiting retrograde ($i = 156.8^\circ$), possessing active nitrogen-ice cryovolcanic geysers powered by subsurface solar greenhouse heating.

## Minor Bodies: Asteroids, Comets, and Trans-Neptunian Objects

Remnants from the accretion era preserve primordial planetary ingredients across three reservoirs:

- **The Main Asteroid Belt ($2.1 - 3.3\text{ AU}$):** Primitive rocky and metallic planetesimals prevented from coalescing into a planet by Jupiter's gravitational perturbations.
  - *Taxonomic Classes:* C-type (carbonaceous, primitive, low albedo, $75\%$ of asteroids); S-type (silicate/stony, inner belt); M-type (metallic iron-nickel, remnants of disrupted differentiated cores).
  - *Kirkwood Gaps:* Depletion zones cleared by Jupiter mean-motion resonances (3:1, 5:2, 2:1).
- **Near-Earth Objects (NEOs) and Planetary Defense:** Asteroids perturbed into Earth-crossing orbits (Atiras, Atens, Apollos, Amors). The kinetic deflection of Dimorphos by NASA's DART mission (2022) demonstrated the feasibility of orbital deflection for planetary defense against hazardous impactors.
- **The Kuiper Belt and Trans-Neptunian Objects (TNOs, $30 - 55\text{ AU}$):** A disc of icy planetesimals:
  - *Classical Kuiper Belt (Cold/Hot):* Unperturbed orbits beyond Neptune.
  - *Resonant Objects (Plutinos):* Bodies locked in Neptune's 3:2 mean-motion resonance (including Pluto).
  - *Scattered Disk Objects (SDOs):* Highly eccentric, inclined bodies scattered outward by Neptune, serving as the reservoir for short-period Jupiter-family comets.
- **The Oort Cloud ($2,000 - 100,000\text{ AU}$):** A vast, spherical cloud of icy planetesimals ejected from the giant-planet region during early migration. Galactic tides and passing stars perturb orbits, sending long-period comets into the inner Solar System.
- **Cometary Activity:** Comets are "dirty snowballs" composed of water ice, frozen gases, organic compounds, and silicates. As a comet approaches perihelion ($r \lesssim 3\text{ AU}$), solar heating sublimates ices, releasing dust to form an expanding **coma**. Solar radiation pressure pushes micron-sized dust into a curved, whitish **dust tail**, while the magnetized solar wind sweeps ionized gas into a straight, bluish **ion tail** pointed directly antisunward.

## The Exoplanetary Bridge: Generalizing Planetary Science

The discovery of thousands of exoplanets by missions like Kepler and TESS demonstrates that the Solar System is only one architecture among a diverse cosmic landscape:

- **Exoplanetary Architectures:**
  - *Hot Jupiters:* Gas giants orbiting within $\sim 0.05\text{ AU}$ of host stars ($P \sim 1-4\text{ days}$), demonstrating large-scale orbital migration that did not happen in the Solar System.
  - *Super-Earths and Sub-Neptunes:* Planets with sizes intermediate between Earth and Neptune ($1.5 - 3.5 R_\oplus$). These are the most common planets in the galaxy, yet entirely absent from our Solar System.
- **The Fulton Radius Valley:** A distinct bi-modal gap in exoplanet radius distributions between $1.5 R_\oplus$ and $2.0 R_\oplus$. Stars photoevaporate the thin hydrogen-helium envelopes of close-in sub-Neptunes, stripping them into bare, rocky Super-Earth cores.
- **Atmospheric Characterization:** Planetary science principles apply directly to exoplanetary atmospheres observed via transmission and emission spectroscopy (using JWST), detecting $\text{H}_2\text{O}, \text{CO}_2, \text{CO}, \text{CH}_4$, and silicate clouds, setting the stage for future biosignature searches on temperate terrestrial exoplanets.
