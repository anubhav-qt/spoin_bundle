## The Shift to Test-Time Compute: Scaling at Inference Time
Throughout the early era of large language models, performance scaling focused almost exclusively on the pre-training phase: scaling parameter counts, dataset sizes, and pre-training FLOPs according to the Kaplan and Chinchilla power laws. However, for complex formal reasoning—including competitive mathematics, systems architecture, competitive programming, and formal scientific verification—pre-training scaling began exhibiting diminishing marginal returns. A fundamental paradigm shift emerged in 2024 and 2025, pioneered by OpenAI’s o1 and o3 models and formalized in academic research by Charlie Snell and colleagues: scaling **Test-Time Compute** (also designated as Inference-Time Compute). Test-time compute represents a new scaling law where allocating additional computational cycles during the generation phase—allowing the model to "think," deliberate, and search through candidate reasoning paths before emitting a final answer—dramatically enhances accuracy. Empirical benchmarks demonstrate that a moderately sized model allowed to spend significant test-time compute exploring reasoning trees consistently outperforms a dense model ten times its size executing standard single-pass greedy decoding. This shift mirrors Daniel Kahneman’s cognitive distinction between "System 1" (rapid, instinctive, heuristic pattern matching) and "System 2" (deliberative, analytical, multi-step logical reasoning).

## Cognitive Dynamics: Internal Chains of Thought and Autonomous Self-Correction
Reasoning models achieve their cognitive breakthroughs by generating extensive, hidden internal Chains of Thought (CoT) prior to synthesizing their user-visible response. Unlike traditional few-shot prompting where humans manually supply step-by-step demonstrations, frontier reasoning models learn to structure their internal monologue autonomously through large-scale reinforcement learning. The internal chain of thought exhibits sophisticated cognitive dynamics: the model decomposes complex problems into smaller, tractable sub-goals, tests edge cases, and verifies mathematical boundary constraints. Crucially, reasoning models exhibit autonomous **Backtracking and Self-Correction**. When an exploration trajectory encounters a logical contradiction, the model generates internal meta-cognitive realizations (e.g., *"Wait, this quadratic factor yields a negative discriminant, which contradicts the real-number constraint. Let me step back and re-evaluate the initial substitution"*), abandoning the compromised branch and exploring an alternative logical pathway. In DeepSeek-R1, researchers observed the spontaneous emergence of "aha moments" during pure reinforcement learning, where the model independently learned to allocate more thinking tokens to difficult problems and verify its intermediate working without explicit human demonstrations.

## Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and Efficient RL
The breakthrough in democratizing reasoning model training was achieved by DeepSeek through the invention of **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**, introduced in DeepSeek-Math and scaled in DeepSeek-R1. Traditional reinforcement learning algorithms for LLMs, most notably Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), suffer from a massive memory bottleneck: PPO requires maintaining a separate Critic (value function) model in GPU memory alongside the Actor (policy) model to estimate state values. Because the Critic model is typically of identical parameter scale to the Policy model, PPO effectively doubles memory and compute requirements, rendering RL on 600B+ parameter models financially and physically prohibitive. GRPO resolves this by eliminating the Critic model entirely. For each training prompt $q$, GRPO samples a group of $G$ candidate outputs $\{o_1, o_2, \dots, o_G\}$ generated by the current policy. The reward model or verifier scores each output, and the baseline reward is computed dynamically as the empirical mean of the group. The advantage of each individual output is evaluated relatively against the group: $A_i = \frac{r_i - \text{mean}(r)}{\text{std}(r)}$. By optimizing policy parameters against this relative group advantage while clipping probability ratios and penalizing KL divergence from the reference model, GRPO slashes training memory by over 50%, enabling efficient, stable end-to-end reinforcement learning across massive clusters.

## Reward Systems: Outcome Verifiers vs. Process Reward Models (PRMs)
The efficacy of reinforcement learning on reasoning depends critically upon the design of the reward signal. In generative tasks, reward mechanisms are divided into Outcome-Supervised Reward Models (ORMs) and Process-Supervised Reward Models (PRMs). An **Outcome Reward Model (ORM)** evaluates only the terminal state of the generation (e.g., did the final numerical answer match the ground truth, or did the compiled code pass all unit tests?). While easy to automate, ORMs suffer from false positives: a model may pursue a completely flawed, hallucinated reasoning chain that accidentally arrives at the correct final integer, rewarding bad reasoning habits. In contrast, a **Process Reward Model (PRM)**, formalized by Hunter Lightman and OpenAI researchers in "Let's Verify Step by Step," evaluates and scores every single individual step of the reasoning chain. PRMs reward sound logical deductions and penalize errors the precise instant they occur, guiding search algorithms (such as Monte Carlo Tree Search - MCTS or Best-of-$N$) along valid reasoning trajectories. In technical domains like competitive programming and formal mathematics, training leverages deterministic **Rule-Based Verifiers**—such as automated test runners (pytest), Python sandboxes, and interactive theorem provers (Lean 4)—which provide infallible, binary ground-truth feedback, completely eliminating human evaluation bias and preventing reward hacking.

## Benchmarking and Frontiers: OpenAI o1/o3 and DeepSeek-R1 Paradigms
The competition between proprietary frontier architectures and open-weight ecosystems has redefined the state of the art in reasoning capabilities. OpenAI’s proprietary **o1** and **o3** series established unprecedented records on elite human intellectual benchmarks, achieving gold-medal standard performance on the International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO), surpassing 95th percentile human ratings on competitive coding platforms (Codeforces), and demonstrating PhD-level competence across physics, chemistry, and biology evaluations. Concurrently, the release of **DeepSeek-R1** in early 2025 demonstrated that these advanced reasoning capabilities could be replicated within an open-weights architecture using large-scale Mixture of Experts (MoE) and pure reinforcement learning. DeepSeek-R1 demonstrated that reasoning behavior can be distilled effectively: by capturing the internal chains of thought generated by a massive 671B reasoning model and using them to fine-tune compact, dense models (such as Qwen 2.5 and Llama 3 8B/70B), smaller edge models inherit profound reasoning and self-correction behaviors without requiring massive test-time compute budgets, establishing a new frontier for accessible, high-intelligence edge computing.
